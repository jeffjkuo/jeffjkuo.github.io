Research Statement

My research examines the political economy of trade agreements, with particular emphasis on Taiwan's role in the global economy. I work across economic modeling, computational economics, and econometrics, with special interest in the impacts of preferential trade agreements and the behavior of multinational firms under globalization.

I strongly believe that politics and economics are inseparable. This conviction has motivated me to combine academic research with policy-facing work. Having opportunities to conduct research, teach international economics, and consult with government agencies continues to shape my agenda as an economist.

Since I witnessed Taiwan's economic transformation under export-oriented development, I have focused on how growth, distribution, and policy adjustment interact in open economies. In one project at Academia Sinica, we revisited wage rigidity in Taiwan's labor market and found that public narratives about the timing and causes of wage stagnation could be improved through clearer empirical evidence.

Another central thread in my work studies how regional integration can generate both aggregate gains and social frictions. While multilateralism and integration often improve efficiency, they may also intensify inequality and political backlash. My goal is to identify these mechanisms by estimating empirically grounded models and by developing theoretical frameworks that characterize political-economic equilibria in open economies.
