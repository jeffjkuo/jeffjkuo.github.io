Research Statement

My research examines the political economy of trade agreements, with particular emphasis on Taiwan's role in the global economy. I work across economic modeling, computational economics, and econometrics, with special interest in the impacts of preferential trade agreements, the behavior of multinational firms under globalization, and the broader interaction among trade, technological change, and geopolitical adjustment.

I strongly believe that politics and economics are inseparable. This conviction has motivated me to combine academic research with policy-facing work. Although I also write in Chinese, I continue to use English as my primary language of professional communication because it remains the most effective medium for scholarly exchange and for engaging with new research, methodologies, and technologies. Having opportunities to conduct research, teach international economics, and consult with government agencies continues to shape my agenda as an economist.

Since I witnessed Taiwan's economic transformation under export-oriented development, I have focused on how growth, distribution, and policy adjustment interact in open economies. In one project at Academia Sinica, we revisited wage rigidity in Taiwan's labor market and found that public narratives about the timing and causes of wage stagnation could be improved through clearer empirical evidence. My current work extends this policy-oriented empirical interest through projects on input-output table analysis and the revision of Taiwan's food balance sheet, both of which aim to strengthen the measurement foundations used in economic analysis and public decision-making.

Another central thread in my work studies how regional integration can generate both aggregate gains and social frictions. While multilateralism and integration often improve efficiency, they may also intensify inequality and political backlash. This perspective also informs my latest publication, which comments on and analyzes the potential economic impact on Taiwan following the 2026 meeting between Donald J. Trump and Xi Jinping in Beijing. My goal is to identify these mechanisms by estimating empirically grounded models and by developing theoretical frameworks that characterize political-economic equilibria in open economies.
